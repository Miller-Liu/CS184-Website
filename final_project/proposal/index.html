Project Overview

From what we've covered in class, light is only modeled as interacting with surfaces. However, in the real world, light also interacts with the space between objects—scattering through the air, diffusing in fog, or breaking into different wavelengths when passing through certain materials. The goal of our project is to enhance realism in rendered scenes by implementing volumetric scattering, allowing us to simulate effects like atmospheric haze, light shafts, and dust-filled environments.

Goals and Deliverables

We plan to deliver scenes with basic volumetric lighting. We want to render images of scenes (like the one below) with light shining through a window or through a projector. Our project will showcase the scene with different parameters like the placement, brightness, and colors of light, as well as the density of fog.

If things go well, we would like to incorporate dust particles that float around and glow in the light. This would add even further to the immersion and create an effect that brings our scenes closer to realism. A problem that may arise is animating the paths of the dust particles.

Another ambition would be to figure out how to set the light source to mimic an image so that we could see the different colors of the image flying through the volumetric lighting (similar to a movie projector).

In addition, we hope to be able to create an interactive GUI that allows us to change certain parameters of the light, camera, and scene while rendering. However, this would require real-time rendering which would be very difficult to implement.

We can measure the performance fo our system by visually comparing the effect we get from volumetric lighting vs without, and also compare our result to what might be expected in real life. In addition, we can compare our visual output to industry examples such as the disney samples to see how well our algorithm did.
